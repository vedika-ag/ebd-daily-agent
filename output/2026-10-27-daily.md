# EBD Daily — Tuesday, 27 October 2026

👕 **D2C apparel brand ReplyAll raises ₹9.7 crore in pre-Series A round**
Bengaluru performance wear brand ReplyAll raised ₹9.7 crore in a round led by Sadev Ventures and Atom XVII, with ISV, Long View Ventures, existing backers Sparrow Capital and Antler, and angel investors taking part. The funds will go to fabric research, product design, new categories and a bigger retail and community presence. Its first three products sold out, and repeat customers make up 40% of revenue.
*Source: Entrackr, YourStory, VIE Stories, Dealroom*

🏬 **Quick commerce to need 75 million sq ft of urban logistics space by 2030, with franchise models driving the push into Tier II and III cities: CBRE**
CBRE estimates India's quick commerce sector will need about 75 million sq ft of urban logistics space by 2030: about 42 million sq ft for in-city dark stores and 33 million sq ft for mother hubs on city outskirts. The network today is around 7,800 dark stores covering about 27 million sq ft across 100+ cities, and is projected to cross 12,000 stores by 2030. About 65–70% of stores are in the top eight metros, and CBRE expects franchise-led dark stores to drive expansion into cities such as Jaipur, Lucknow, Surat, Coimbatore and Nagpur at lower cost.
*Source: ANI, Economic Times Retail, The Tribune, Zee Business*

💎 **Jacob & Co enters Punjab real estate with ₹2,500 crore Mohali project**
GB Realty plans to invest about ₹2,400–2,500 crore in Punjab's first Jacob & Co-branded residences on Airport Road, Mohali, with over ₹1,000 crore going towards land. Jacob & Co is the design and brand partner, and two towers will carry Salman Khan's name. The project was launched on October 24.
*Source: Business Today, The Tribune, Construction Week India, Babushahi*

💄 **Beauty and personal care sees strong growth this festive season**
India's beauty and personal care market is seeing double-digit growth this festive season, driven by ecommerce and quick commerce demand. Premium skincare, fragrances and men's grooming are leading, and shoppers are trading up to higher-value products, including international premium brands.
*Source: The Hindu BusinessLine, Whalesbook*

📉 **Snapdeal parent AceVector's Q2 loss rises 116% to ₹16.2 crore; revenue up 17%**
AceVector's September-quarter net loss rose 116% year on year to ₹16.2 crore from ₹7.5 crore, though it narrowed 37% from ₹25.5 crore in the June quarter. Operating revenue grew 17% year on year to ₹151.2 crore, and the adjusted EBITDA loss widened to ₹8.6 crore from ₹2.2 crore. The company cited festive marketing spend, logistics cost pressure at Snapdeal and investment in Unicommerce.
*Source: Inc42, Entrackr, Investing.com*

🌿 **Kapiva crosses ₹460 crore revenue in FY26, targets EBITDA profitability by Q4 FY27**
Kapiva's revenue from operations grew 36.5% to ₹464 crore in FY26 from ₹340 crore, while its loss stayed broadly flat at ₹72 crore against ₹69 crore. Marketplace revenue rose 34% and its D2C channel grew 21%. The company says its annual recurring revenue is about ₹650–700 crore and it expects positive EBITDA by Q4 FY27.
*Source: Entrackr*

☕ **Trent to bring Caffe Nero to Westside and Zudio stores**
Trent has signed a licensing deal to open Caffe Nero coffee shops inside its Westside and Zudio outlets, after talks with Tata Starbucks ended over store format issues. Trent and Caffe Nero did not comment, and no rollout timeline or store count has been disclosed.
*Source: Economic Times, Whalesbook*

⚖️ **Consumer commission orders Zepto to pay ₹57,000 for charging ₹34 above MRP**
The Ranga Reddy District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission held that Zepto charged ₹184 for a product with a ₹150 MRP, after showing a discount on an inflated ₹214 price. It ruled that charging above MRP and discounting an inflated price is an unfair trade practice, and rejected Zepto's defence that it was an unintended pricing error.
*Source: Indian Express, The420.in, Hyderabad Mail*

🛵 **Swiggy's discount dilemma returns: growth or profitability?**
Instamart's discounts, including brand-funded ones, rose to 29% of order value in Q2 from 26% in the previous quarter, months after Swiggy said it would compete on differentiation rather than price. Net order value grew 13.4% quarter on quarter to ₹6,594 crore, but Instamart's adjusted EBITDA loss widened to ₹895 crore from ₹778 crore, and its contribution margin slipped back to −1% of gross order value.
*Source: Business Today, Inc42*

🤖 **Brands turn to AI-generated ads this festive season**
Indian brands are using AI to create, test and personalise festive campaigns to cut time and cost, with OPPO, SBI Life Insurance and Haier among those skipping studio shoots. Festive advertising makes up roughly a third of India's annual ad spend of about ₹60,000–62,000 crore, per Datum Intelligence. Ferns N Petals says its creative execution sped up nearly 4X, and RENÉE Cosmetics reports a roughly 30% engagement lift.
*Source: Mint, NewsBytes, Whalesbook*

🥨 **Snacking brand Troovy raises ₹100 crore**
Gurugram-based clean-label snack brand Troovy has raised ₹100 crore in a round led by Fireside Ventures and Sharrp Ventures. The money will fund manufacturing expansion and product development as it grows in retail and quick commerce.
*Source: Economic Times Retail, Whalesbook*

🏦 **Why Walmart would rather keep Flipkart private, for now**
Walmart has asked Flipkart to put its IPO on hold and reach EBITDA breakeven by the end of FY27, with no listing or pre-IPO round expected until then. Flipkart moved its parent entity from Singapore to India in 2025 to prepare for a listing, which some reports now put around 2028.
*Source: Economic Times Retail, Outlook Business, Moneycontrol*

🛍️ **Myntra's festive orders nearly double as shoppers beyond metros drive demand**
Myntra's Big Fashion Festival recorded nearly double the orders of regular business days for D2C brands, international labels and its Gen Z platform FWD. New customers from non-metro regions rose 74%, led by Guwahati, Dehradun, Bhubaneswar, Jammu and Gorakhpur, and sports footwear orders rose 3.5 times. Visits rose 25% over the previous edition.
*Source: Economic Times Retail, Apparel Resources, Daily Excelsior*
