Title: Haleon in Talks to Buy HealthKart at Up to $1.4 Bn Valuation, Supreme Court Gives FSSAI Four Months for Warning Labels & Swiggy Instamart Cuts 340 Jobs

Excerpt: Haleon is in early talks to take control of HealthKart at a $1.2–1.4 billion valuation, the Supreme Court gives FSSAI four months to notify front-of-pack warning labels with a year for companies to comply, and Instamart cuts 340 corporate roles as its gross order value reaches ₹9,230 crore.

Description:

**Haleon in talks to buy a controlling stake in HealthKart at up to $1.4 Bn valuation**
The early-stage talks would value HealthKart at $1.2–1.4 Bn, and a full sale is also being considered, according to Mint. HealthKart, which owns MuscleBlaze, HK Vitals and Gritzo, is also exploring an IPO. It runs more than 200 stores across over 90 cities.
*Source: Inc42, Mint*

**Supreme Court gives FSSAI four months to notify front-of-pack warning labels**
After notification, food companies get one year to comply, which counts as a voluntary implementation period. Each of high sugar, high saturated fat and high salt must have its own red hexagon, with a font that is "clear, prominent and readily legible".
*Source: ET Retail*

**Swiggy's Instamart to lay off 340 employees as part of a restructuring**
The cuts affect corporate and support roles. Swiggy says the aim is to remove overlapping roles and use more generative AI, while it keeps investing in dark stores and frontline staff. Instamart's gross order value grew 16.7% from the previous quarter to ₹9,230 Cr in Q2 FY27, and its contribution margin improved to -1% of GOV.
*Source: Economic Times, Entrackr, ET Retail*

**Maharashtra FDA fines Blinkit ₹11 lakh and suspends Zepto and BigBasket licences**
The state FDA, led by Tukaram Mundhe, suspended 20 licences after hygiene raids on quick commerce facilities. Mid-Day reports that inspectors found cockroaches at a Zepto dark store and rats at a BigBasket warehouse.
*Source: NewsBytes, The Statesman, Mid-Day*

**Black Friday arrives 19 days after Diwali this year, against 39 days last year**
The four-day Black Friday–Cyber Monday window runs until 30 November. It follows a festive season in which online sales rose about 17% to ₹1.41 lakh Cr, short of Datum Intelligence's ₹1.50–1.55 lakh Cr forecast. Last year, daily GMV on GoKwik's network rose 42% during the window and Unicommerce saw about 27% YoY growth in Black Friday week orders.
*Source: Inc42*

**US Black Friday online sales set to cross $13 Bn: Adobe**
US online spending reached $9.7 Bn by 6:30 pm ET on Black Friday, up 12.8% year on year. Adobe expects the day to close at $13–13.4 Bn and Cyber Monday to bring in $15.1 Bn. It projects $47.5 Bn across the five days from Thanksgiving to Cyber Monday, up 7.4%.
*Source: ET Retail*

**Beauty and personal care brands top influencer ad violations: ASCI**
ASCI flagged 456 BPC cases in April–September 2026, mainly unsubstantiated acne, pigmentation and hairfall claims and exaggerated before-and-after visuals. Of 3,951 complaints, 2,308 violated the ASCI Code, and 97.93% of violations were in digital media. 58% of the cases that brands didn't contest involved missing influencer disclosures.
*Source: Inc42*

**Foxtale's revenue nearly doubles to ₹382.6 Cr in FY26, loss narrows 10%**
Operating revenue rose 93% from ₹198.7 Cr, and total income reached ₹400 Cr. Ad spend grew 68.5% to ₹178.3 Cr, or 38% of total costs, and the net loss narrowed to ₹66.1 Cr. Foxtale spent ₹1.22 to earn each rupee of revenue.
*Source: Entrackr*

**indē wild slips into a loss in FY26 despite a 2.4x jump in revenue**
The Nikhil Kamath-backed BPC brand's operating revenue rose 139% to ₹59.8 Cr. Expenses grew more than 2.5x, turning an FY25 profit of ₹38.5 lakh into a ₹95 lakh loss. Sephora stocks the brand in 197 stores across the US and UK.
*Source: Inc42*

**Rapido's Ownly to charge Bengaluru customers a delivery fee from 30 November**
Orders delivered beyond 7 km will carry a ₹70 fee, while restaurants stay on zero commission and their payouts are unchanged. This is Ownly's second pricing revision in under a year. It earlier added a ₹25 fee on Bengaluru orders below ₹99.
*Source: The Hindu BusinessLine, Inc42*

**TRAI extends 1601-series numbers to ecommerce, hospitals, airlines and others**
In the second phase of the 1601 series, ecommerce platforms, retail chains, food delivery, ride-hailing, airlines and hospitals can use the numbers for service and transaction calls, but not promotional ones. Eligibility is limited to companies with at least ₹500 Cr annual turnover in one of the last three years. Telecom operators have 180 days to onboard them.
*Source: The Hindu BusinessLine, The 420*

**Global fashion brands shift orders from Bangladesh to India amid unrest**
Between 1 and 18 November, brands including Zara, H&M and Marks & Spencer diverted about $254 Mn of garment orders from Bangladesh, mostly to India, after labour unrest and energy shortages. BGMEA puts production losses at over $400 Mn. Bangladesh has approved a 36% minimum-wage rise for garment workers.
*Source: Business Standard, ET Retail*

**Flipkart's new bets face a slowdown as CEO Kalyan Krishnamurthy exits**
The Economic Times reports that Flipkart's newer businesses are slowing as Krishnamurthy prepares to leave. He is stepping down after more than 11 years, and Flipkart has said it will name a successor in the coming weeks.
*Source: Economic Times*

**Brooks running shoes return to India after 2.5 years, launching online with Flipkart**
The US running-shoe brand is returning to the Indian market after two and a half years. Its relaunch is online, through Flipkart.
*Source: Business Standard, The Wire*

**Quick commerce is changing what India drinks**
In an ET Retail column, the maker of Lahori Zeera writes that quick commerce is giving functional drinks, kombucha, flavoured diet drinks and mixers a discovery channel that traditional stores do not. It notes that platform commissions, warehousing and ad costs make the channel hard for low-priced drinks. It also says shoppers' loyalty often stays with the platform rather than the brand.
*Source: ET Retail*

**NUUK expands its home appliances portfolio, targets ₹150 Cr revenue in FY27**
The design-led appliance brand, founded in 2023, now has 20 products and variants across cooling, floor care, food preparation, heating and garment care. It has sold more than 6 lakh units through its website, marketplaces and quick commerce, and reported net revenue of about ₹70 Cr in FY26.
*Source: Inc42*

**Blue Star and Voltas shares fall up to 3% as Urban Company enters the AC market**
AC makers' shares slipped on Friday after Urban Company opened pre-bookings for its Native ACs, priced from ₹55,499. The ACs are sold only on its app and come with a five-year unconditional warranty that includes gas refills.
*Source: Moneycontrol, CNBC TV18, Inc42*

**Amazon workers protest over low wages and short-term contracts in 25 cities**
Amazon warehouse and delivery workers in India joined the global "Make Amazon Pay" protests on Black Friday. They demanded better wages, an end to short-term contracts, and social security.
*Source: Business Standard, The Hindu BusinessLine*

**Delhivery starts arbitration against Ecom Express's former investors over ₹51.88 Cr**
Delhivery filed a notice with the Singapore International Arbitration Centre against British International Investment, Warburg Pincus' Eaglebay Investment and PG Esmeralda. It is seeking indemnity for losses found after it bought Ecom Express for ₹1,407 Cr in July 2025. The respondents disputed the claims.
*Source: Entrackr*

**Swara Baby Products IPO opens on 2 December**
The FirstCry-backed hygiene maker's ₹800 Cr issue, at ₹105–111 a share, closes on 4 December and lists on 9 December.
*Source: Entrackr, YourStory*
