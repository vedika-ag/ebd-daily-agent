Title: Quick Commerce Festive Orders Jump 75%, B2C Ecommerce Funding Falls 33% to $1.6 Bn & Meesho Completes Kirana Club Buy

Excerpt: Quick commerce order volumes rose 75% in the festive period as overall ecommerce orders grew 21%, B2C ecommerce funding fell 33% to $1.6 billion this year, and Meesho completed a ₹186.71 crore buy of 92.74% of Kirana Club. Also: D2C websites' 27% order jump, Zepto's 14,000 coins an hour, Sugar Cosmetics' smaller loss, Coupang's $220 million fine, FMCG cost-cutting and Redseer's $16 billion festive estimate.

Description:

**Quick commerce orders jump 75% as ecommerce and quick commerce firms widen festive offerings**
Overall ecommerce order volumes grew 21% year on year in the peak festive period from 6 October to 4 November, according to Unicommerce data. Quick commerce led with a 75% rise in order volumes and 38% growth in GMV, while D2C brand websites grew orders 30% and marketplaces 8%. Platforms widened their range beyond essentials into gold coins, sweets, diyas, décor, fashion and gift hampers.
*Source: Business Standard, Pressbee*

**India's B2C ecommerce funding falls 33% to $1.6 Bn in 2026: Tracxn**
Indian B2C ecommerce startups raised about $1.6 Bn across 356 rounds between 1 January and 3 November 2026, down 33% from $2.4 Bn across 621 rounds a year earlier. Late-stage funding fell 51% to $587 Mn and seed funding fell 50% to $169 Mn, while early-stage funding rose 3% to $872 Mn. Acquisitions fell to 19 from 25, and IPOs nearly halved to 7.
*Source: Business Standard, Storyboard18, SME Street, YourStory*

**Meesho completes majority stake acquisition in Kirana Club and Retail Pulse Labs**
Meesho has bought a 92.74% stake in Kirana Club and its Indian subsidiary Retail Pulse Labs for ₹186.71 Cr, completing the first two of three tranches. Kirana Club builds software and community tools for grocers, while Retail Pulse Labs runs a B2B marketplace connecting kiranas and small merchants with FMCG brands and distributors in Tier 2 to 4 towns and rural markets. The final tranche is due by 31 March 2027.
*Source: CNBC-TV18, NewsBytes, IndiaIPO*

**D2C brands see 27% more orders ahead of Diwali as sales value rises 45%**
ClickPost's analysis of more than 8 crore orders across about 400 D2C brand websites found daily orders up 27% ahead of Diwali, with sales value up 45%. Hyperlocal delivery on brand websites rose to 5% of orders from 2.6% last year. Kearney estimates D2C customer acquisition costs rose 15% to 20%, to about ₹600 to ₹650.
*Source: Business Standard, Lapaas Voice*

**Online festive sales set to rise 25% to about $16 Bn in the run-up to Diwali**
Online sales in the run-up to Diwali were expected to rise about 25% to roughly $16 Bn (₹1.4 lakh crore), according to Redseer, as reported by Bloomberg. Redseer had projected ₹1.15 lakh crore for last year's festive period.
*Source: Business Today*

**Zepto delivered over 14,000 gold and silver coins an hour as Diwali shopping shifts to quick commerce**
Zepto delivered more than 14,000 gold and silver coins in its busiest hour on Dhanteras, about 298 coins a minute, according to co-founder and CEO Aadit Palicha. Swiggy Instamart said bullion orders rose up to 23 times on Dhanteras, and one shopper spent ₹1.73 lakh on gold coins in a single order.
*Source: Economic Times, WION, Retail News Asia*

**Sugar Cosmetics cuts losses 41% in FY26 as revenue dips 11%**
Sugar Cosmetics' parent Sugar Brands cut its net loss 41% to ₹79.3 Cr in FY26 from ₹134.3 Cr, while revenue from operations fell 10.7% to ₹361 Cr from ₹404.4 Cr. Total expenses fell 18.9% to ₹443 Cr, helped by lower spending on ads and salaries. Cash and bank balances fell to ₹10.30 Cr from ₹23 Cr.
*Source: Entrackr, NewsBytes, VIE Stories*

**Consumer goods companies turn to cost-cutting as input prices rise**
Consumer goods companies are turning to cost-cutting as input prices rise, to protect demand and revive volume growth. Crude, palm oil and packaging inflation was expected to squeeze FMCG margins in Q2 FY27, with Dabur and Godrej Consumer flagging inflation. Marico was an exception: its material costs rose only about 1% on 13% revenue growth, helped by lower copra prices.
*Source: Economic Times, Retail Intel, Kotak Neo*

**South Korea fines Coupang $220 Mn for abusing dominance in food delivery**
South Korea's Fair Trade Commission has fined Coupang 300.8 billion won (about $220 Mn) for making restaurants match the better terms they offered on rival delivery apps and penalising those that didn't, between July 2023 and January 2026. The regulator also imposed a provisional fine of 331.1 billion won on Baemin operator Woowa Brothers. Coupang called the penalty excessive.
*Source: Reuters, The Korea Times*

**Diwali is over; now comes the real quick commerce test for brands**
After Diwali, brands are weighing what quick commerce actually delivered, amid higher visibility, rising advertising costs and questions about how much of the sales were incremental. With platforms controlling transactions and customer data, brands are being pushed to fold quick commerce into wider omnichannel plans.
*Source: exchange4media*

**Three Bihar friends build a ₹2.5 Cr business from banana waste**
Taruwar Agro, started in 2021 by MBA graduates Jagat Kalyan, Satyam Kumar and Nitish Kumar Verma, turns fibre from discarded banana plants into everyday products such as yoga mats, file folders, baskets and prayer mats. The company reported about ₹2.5 Cr in revenue in FY26. The founders started with about ₹20 lakh from personal and family savings.
*Source: Moneycontrol, YourStory*
